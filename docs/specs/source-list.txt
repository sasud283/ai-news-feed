All have public RSS feeds with episode titles, show notes, and descriptions — sufficient for classification without needing full audio transcription. Transcription (e.g. via a speech-to-text API) would allow deeper summarization but is a meaningfully bigger cost and complexity addition, and is treated as a future enhancement rather than an MVP requirement.
Note on Spotify specifically: most shows above are also distributed on Spotify, but that's irrelevant to ingestion — what matters is whether a show has an open RSS feed, which most independent podcasts do regardless of where they're also listened to. Spotify-exclusive shows with no public RSS are a genuine gap: reaching those would need Spotify's own API, which is built for personalized playback rather than full-catalog indexing and would likely require partner-level access rather than a simple feed pull. Not worth building around at MVP — flag it as a known gap rather than a blocker.

One-off Documentaries
AI-focused documentaries and investigative long-form video are released rarely and irregularly — there's no equivalent to an RSS feed producing a steady stream of these. This category is realistically a manually curated addition: the operator adds a documentary or long-form piece to the site directly (through the same interface as the spot-check queue) when one is released, rather than the automated pipeline discovering it. Likely sources to monitor periodically: major streaming documentary units (Netflix, Bloomberg Originals), broadcast investigative strands (PBS Frontline, BBC), and long-form YouTube channels doing AI explainers or investigations. Given the low volume, this content type should be expected to update monthly or less, not daily.
